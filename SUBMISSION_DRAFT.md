# Submission text (draft: edit to fit the NASA form fields)

**Project title:** Wayanad from orbit: before and after the Kalladi slide

**Challenge:** Dancing with the SARs

**Summary (short):**
NISAR radar sees through monsoon cloud, but its images are hard for ordinary people to read. We built an interactive page that compares NISAR L-band radar previews of Wayanad, Kerala, before (27 June 2026) and after (9 July 2026) the 7 July Kalladi landslide, adds an interferogram spanning the event, and explains in plain language what radar can and cannot show.

**What we did:**
We searched the ASF archive for NISAR provisional products over Wayanad, chose scenes on the same repeating track around the event, aligned two previews using their footprint files, and built a swipe comparison with honest limits.

**Physical drivers we considered:**
Monsoon rainfall changes soil moisture and vegetation water content, which changes radar backscatter on its own. A slope failure changes surface roughness and structure. The page explains why two dates alone cannot separate these, and proposes testing whether a change persists across later dates (21 Jul, 14 Aug, 26 Aug).

**Polarizations:**
The previews are colour composites of the radar's polarizations. Cross-polarized (HV) signal is generally more sensitive to vegetation structure, so it is the natural next layer to analyse. We have not yet done that quantitatively.

**Limits we state openly:**
Quick-look previews, not calibrated backscatter. Different frames of the same track. Provisional data. The landslide itself is not visible at this scale.

**Future applications:**
Calibrated multi-date change detection on slopes; recovery tracking as more NISAR data accrues; combining radar change with road and settlement layers for exposure context, without claiming landslide prediction.
